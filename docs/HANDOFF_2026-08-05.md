## 2. The diagnosis that framed everything

GeroQuery was a **snapshot with excellent provenance**. Three properties
separate a research artifact from research infrastructure, and it had none:

| Property | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| **A heartbeat** | Corpus frozen at 31 GEO DataSets selected 2026-08-03 | Monthly rebuild that publishes what moved (§4) |
| **Reachability** | An API on `localhost` a human must clone and build | MCP server + a static site on a CDN (§5, §6) |
| **A citable result** | Three real results, none with a hard outcome | 2,517 people, 1,350 deaths, 20 years (§3) |

The strategic point: the Biomarkers of Aging Consortium names its own blockers
as *"limited reproducibility across cohorts, inconsistent dataset structures,
and insufficient validation against hard clinical end points."* GeroQuery was
already the first two. The third is now done.

## 3. The cross-layer result — §10.3 was not blocked

`HANDOFF.md` §10.3 called this "the reason this exists" and listed the obstacle
as clocks and health state living in different cohorts. **That obstacle stopped
existing in July 2024 and nobody had noticed.** NCHS released per-participant
DNAm biomarkers for NHANES 1999–2002 on 2024-07-31, and those participants are
in the same survey — and the same linked mortality file — as the clinical panel.

### The cohort

**n = 2,517 · 1,350 deaths · 17.1 y median follow-up (max 20.8) · age 50–85.**

Eleven newly pinned artifacts, joined on `SEQN`:

| Group | Files | What |
|---|---|---|
| `dnmepi.sas7bdat` | 1 | 12 NCHS-computed DNAm clocks + 6 cell fractions + a DNAm survey weight |
| NHANES 1999–2002 | 8 | Demographics, biochemistry, CBC, CRP — both cycles |
| NCHS linked mortality | 2 | Follow-up through 2019-12-31, fixed-width |

`geroquery/sources/nhanes_dnam.py` (~560 lines) does the join.
`geroquery/etl/build_crosslayer.py` writes the table. `make crosslayer`.

### The estimator

`geroquery/survival/` — a new peer of `harmonize/` and `resilience/`:

- **Cox proportional hazards**, Breslow ties, Newton-Raphson with backtracking
- **Harrell's C**
- **Nested likelihood-ratio tests** — the only way to answer "does either add
  anything over the other"
- **Mahalanobis dysregulation** — the per-subject analogue of CSD, since CSD is
  a population statistic and cannot enter a Cox model

Hand-rolled in numpy + scipy; neither lifelines nor statsmodels is a dependency.
It earns trust three ways in `tests/test_survival.py`:

1. **A closed form.** Three subjects, x = [0, 1, 0], times 1 < 2 < 3: the
   Breslow MLE is exactly β = ln(2)/2. Matched to 1e-9.
2. **Finite differences.** The analytic gradient and Hessian agree with the
   numerical derivatives of the log partial likelihood they claim to
   differentiate, to 1e-6. This catches a risk set accumulated in the wrong
   direction — a bug that otherwise produces a smooth, convergent, wrong fit.
3. **A planted effect.** Simulated survival with known [0.7, −0.4] recovers to
   ±0.06. Residual bias over 40 replicates is ~2% relative, consistent with the
   known O(1/n) bias of the Cox MLE rather than an implementation error.

### The findings

**The published clock hierarchy reproduces from raw files by code that was never
told it** — mortality-trained > phenotype-trained > chronological-age-trained:

| Clock | Trained on | HR/SD (adjusted) | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| GrimAge2 | mortality | **1.443** | 1.361–1.531 |
| GrimAge | mortality | **1.427** | 1.346–1.512 |
| PhenoAge | clinical phenotype | **1.163** | 1.099–1.231 |
| Horvath | chronological age | **1.129** | 1.069–1.192 |
| Hannum | chronological age | **1.108** | 1.049–1.171 |
| Lin | chronological age | 1.052 | **0.998–1.108** |
| SkinBlood | chronological age | 1.049 | **0.992–1.108** |
| Weidner | chronological age | 1.026 | **0.976–1.078** |

Getting that ordering wrong would have been the loudest possible signal the join
was scrambled.

**Three clocks do not predict death at all** once dysregulation is in the model.
Published as found.

**The headline:** a Mahalanobis distance over six routine clinical chemistry
markers (**C = 0.757**) beats nine of the ten DNAm clocks and adds information
to all ten, including GrimAge2 (p = 1.5×10⁻¹⁴). Only GrimAge (0.762) and
GrimAge2 (0.764) beat it — both trained on the outcome being predicted. The six
markers cost a blood panel; the clocks cost an EPIC array.

**And the caveat that deflates it, reported next to it rather than in a
footnote:** age and sex alone reach C = 0.7394. Everything above adds **0.034**
of concordance. With 1,350 events a likelihood-ratio test detects effects far
below the size that would change a decision about a person.

**A fourth finding, predicted before it was run.** The population CSD signal
*vanishes* here — variance slope +0.0010, CI [−0.0015, +0.0038], not supported —
where it was supported 20/20 on NHANES 2017–2018. The cause is the age floor of
50 removing half the dynamic range. Yet the *individual* dysregulation statistic
is strongly predictive in the same subjects (Q1→Q4 death rate 44.9% → 69.5%) and
almost uncorrelated with age (r = 0.077). **The two measure different things,
and this is the first cohort that could separate them.**

## 4. The living evidence loop

`geroquery/etl/panel_diff.py` + `.github/workflows/living-evidence.yml`.

Monthly, the GEO selection query re-runs, the panel rebuilds from whatever now
satisfies it, and 41,983 pooled estimates are diffed against a committed
baseline. The output is not "data refreshed" — it is a changelog of **effect
sizes that moved, with intervals**, classified into eight kinds ordered by
newsworthiness (intervals crossing zero first, bookkeeping last).

```
FOXO3   g -0.31 [-0.62, +0.01] k=7   ->  g -0.38 [-0.65, -0.11] k=9   newly excludes zero
CDKN2A  g +0.07 [-0.20, +0.35] k=14  ->  g +0.11 [-0.14, +0.36] k=16  still null
```

Curated aging databases publish *entries*. This publishes *how the evidence
changed*, which is the only honest way to say a claim strengthened.

**Three guardrails, enforced not remembered:**

1. **Nothing auto-merges.** No merge step exists, and
   `test_workflow_never_merges_and_only_ever_opens_a_pull_request` fails the
   build if one is added.
2. **Departures are as prominent as arrivals.** A withdrawn GDS shrinks the
   evidence behind every gene in it.
3. **The panel stays rule-selected.** `panel_diff` observes; it has no mechanism
   to include or exclude a dataset.

Baseline is 862 KB gzipped and **overwritten**, so repo growth is bounded by the
changelog (a few KB/month), not the corpus.

## 5. The MCP server

`geroquery/mcp/` — five tools over the existing service layer. `pip install -e
".[mcp]"`, then `geroquery-mcp`.

MCPmed (*Brief Bioinform* 2026;27(1):bbag076) is an explicit call for
bioinformatics services to expose a machine-actionable layer; Open Targets
shipped one on the same reasoning. The pattern it recommends — a typed API plus
a thin MCP endpoint — is the shape this package already had.

**The substantive argument is narrower than "agents are useful."** Ask a
language model whether CDKN2A changes with age and it answers from the
literature, which is how CDKN2A became the most-cited gene in aging despite
pooling to *g* = +0.07 [−0.20, +0.35] over fourteen contrasts here. The tool
returns the measurement and the interval. That is the whole product.

So every payload carries, unconditionally: a `verdict` (`increases` /
`decreases` / `no_evidence` / `not_measured`), the `interval`, the study count,
and the caveats that apply to *that* answer. The server instructions explicitly
tell a model not to override a `no_evidence` with background knowledge.

`tools.py` imports no SDK, so the payload logic is testable without the extra
installed — the same split as `api/app.py` over `api/service.py`.

## 6. The front end

Vite + React + Observable Plot + hyparquet. No backend, no API, no runtime
dependency beyond a CDN. Four views: gene readout with forest plot, whole-corpus
effect distribution, the rule-selected panel, and the mortality result.

### Design

**The zero line is the protagonist.** The product's claim is that an interval
can say "we cannot tell", so x = 0 is a real structural element — drawn heavier
than the axis and every other rule, in every chart. The mortality view's
reference is 1.0 (hazard ratios are multiplicative) but does the same job at the
same weight.

**Type encodes epistemics.** Instrument Serif appears only on assertions. IBM
Plex Sans is the interface. IBM Plex Mono is every measurement, tabular figures
always. Three families because there are three kinds of statement on the page.

**Direction colours are equal in weight.** A gene rising with age is not better
or worse than one falling, so neither gets the "good" colour. The null is a
neutral warm grey, never red: *we cannot tell* is an answer, not an error.

**Filled vs hollow is the verdict grammar** — filled means the interval excludes
the null, hollow means it crosses. Identical in the gene rail, the forest plot,
and the mortality chart.

**URL is the state.** `?gene=CDKN2A&species=human&view=landscape`. A gene result
is finally linkable, which was §10.1's item 6 and the strongest single argument
for leaving Streamlit.

### The architectural line

**Every statistic is computed in Python** by the same estimators the API serves.
JavaScript filters, sorts, bins and draws — it never pools, fits, or derives an
interval. Two implementations of DerSimonian–Laird would drift, and the repo
already keeps `test_matrix_hedges_g_matches_scalar_implementation` because a
vectorized copy of one estimator was worth pinning to its reference.

### I reversed my own recommendation

`ROADMAP.md` §3.1 argued for DuckDB-WASM over Parquet row groups. Cloudflare's
25 MiB per-file limit exposed the flaw: **the wasm binaries are 34 MB and 39 MB,
about seven times the 6.8 MB corpus they would query.** Range requests only pay
when the data dwarfs the engine.

| | engine | requests | transferred | deploys? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DuckDB-WASM | 73 MB | — | — | **no** |
| hyparquet, ranged | ~15 kB | 8 | 7.58 MB | yes |
| **hyparquet, whole-file** | ~15 kB | **2** | **7.06 MB** | yes |

The ranged reader was *also* worse than plain fetches — because every row group
is read anyway, its widening Range GETs overlap and re-fetch. The roadmap has
been corrected in place rather than quietly left wrong.

| # | Bug | Consequence |

| 12 | NHANES `XY_Estimation` is 1=female/2=male — the **inverse** of `RIAGENDR` | Both are valid-looking 1/2 integers, so the naive map gave 2.4% agreement instead of 97.6%: DNAm sex was wrong for **every subject** |

| 18 | Forest-plot labels stripped `GEO:GDS…` to the bare accession | One GDS can yield two contrasts, so both got the same label and **Plot's ordinal scale merged two measurements into one row** |

**#16 is the one worth dwelling on.** I reported it last session as "mypy is
broken." It was worse: because a stub syntax error aborts the run, mypy had been
checking *nothing* — including all nine modules added in `455e9cf`. Silent
because 3.10 and 3.11 resolve an older numpy whose stubs parse fine, so only the

## 10. Gotchas added this session

- **`XY_Estimation` is 1=female, 2=male** — the opposite of `RIAGENDR`. Use
  `DNAM_SEX_LABELS`, never `SEX_LABELS`.
- **NHANES variable names are not stable across cycles.** Check the file's
  actual columns before assuming a 2017–2018 name works in 1999–2002.
- **The two NHANES cohorts are not interchangeable.** 2017–2018 is age 20–80
  with hs-CRP; 1999–2002 is 50–85 with standard CRP, and the age floor removes
  the CSD variance signal entirely.
- **Do not re-pin `python_version` in `[tool.mypy]`.** It is unset on purpose.
- **`geroquery/mcp/tools.py` must never import the MCP SDK.**
- **The front end never computes a statistic.** If you find yourself writing
  DerSimonian–Laird in JS, add it to `etl/build_frontend_data.py` instead.
- **`make idmap` must have run before `make frontend-data`**, or 94% of genes
  render as accessions. The exporter raises if no map exists at all, but cannot
  tell a stale map from a complete one.
- **`frontend/public/data/` is committed** even though derived — it is the
  deployable artifact and Cloudflare cannot run the Python ETL.
- **Never let the living-evidence workflow merge itself**, and never hand-edit
  the GEO panel to accept a diff.
- **`.[dev]` is not enough to run the suite honestly.**

## 11. What is still open

**Highest value, in order:**

1. **No front-end test suite.** Playwright against the built `dist` is the right
   harness. Given this repo has now produced *four* silent UI bugs that a green
   Python suite did not catch (#9, #10, #17, #18), this is the most obviously
   missing thing in the whole project.
2. **Apply survey weights** to the mortality result. `WTDN4YR`, `SDMVPSU` and
   `SDMVSTRA` are all carried and unused. This converts a claim about 2,517
   people into a claim about the US population aged 50+ — the single highest
   value follow-up on §3.
3. **The corpus loads whole** (6.8 MB) on first query. Fine on desktop, slow on
   2G. Splitting `contrasts.parquet` by species halves it.
4. **ARCHS4** — removes the microarray-only limitation *and* the uncorrected
   cross-study heterogeneity in one move. Do it after the living-evidence loop
   has run at least once, so the change is diffed rather than silent.
5. **Nested random effects by series** — 32 contrasts from 27 Series share
   subjects, so pooling slightly overstates independence. A correctness fix that
   changes published numbers.

**Smaller:**

- Cause-specific mortality (`ucod_leading` is carried and unused).
- No proportional-hazards diagnostic over 20 years of follow-up.
- Expose `gene_report`, `list_curated_genes`, `references` over HTTP.
- `?library=` filter on `/v1/clocks` (240 unfiltered entries).
- A second omic layer — `omic_layer` still reads `transcriptome` everywhere.
- Retire the Streamlit dashboard once the static site has full parity. It is
  still running and still correct; nothing forces the switch yet.

## 12. Honest limitations of what shipped

**The mortality result** is age 50–85 only, cross-sectional in exposure,
unweighted, uses NCHS's clocks rather than ours (which we therefore cannot
audit), and uses Breslow ties which attenuate hazard ratios slightly toward the
null. Public-use mortality records are perturbed by NCHS. Full list in
`RESULTS_CROSSLAYER.md` §6.

**The living-evidence loop has never run.** The baseline is established and the
diff logic is tested against synthetic snapshots, but no real monthly rebuild
has happened yet. The first one is the real test.

**The MCP server has not been exercised by an actual agent** — the tools are
tested directly, and the transport binding is not.

**The front end has no automated tests** (see §11.1). It was verified by hand in
a real browser at 1440px and 390px, light and dark, against the built `dist`
rather than the dev server — but "I looked at it" is not a regression suite.
