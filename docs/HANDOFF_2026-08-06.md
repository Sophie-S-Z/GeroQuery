That found two of my own three assertions to be vacuous:

| Test | What was wrong with it |
|---|---|
| Forest plot merges two contrasts (#18) | Fixture was human `CDKN2A`, chosen from memory. **GDS5226 is the only accession in the panel that yields two contrasts, and it is mouse** — human CDKN2A touches no duplicated accession, so the bug could not manifest. | Clock labels (#19) | Asserted labels are unique. Under the bug they *are* unique — `GrimAgeMort`→"Grim", `GrimAge2Mort`→"GrimAge2". Now asserts the contract: a label is its clock name minus at most a literal `Mort` |

Only the accession test (#17) caught its bug unaided. Both weak tests now fail
when their bug is reintroduced and pass when it is not, verified in both
directions.

> The general lesson, worth keeping: **a regression test written from the bug
> report rather than from the data will often not reproduce the bug.** Picking
> the fixture by querying the corpus is what made the difference.

## 3. Survey weights — the mortality result is now a population claim

`WTDN4YR`, `SDMVSTRA` and `SDMVPSU` were carried and unused. They are applied.

**n = 2,517 measured → 75,754,006 US adults aged 50+, 35,727,943 deaths.**

### What was built

`survival/cox.py` gained a survey path, roughly 90 lines:

- **Binder (1992) weighted pseudo-likelihood.** Weights enter both the risk-set
  sums and the event terms.
- **Taylor-linearized design variance** over strata × PSU, with the Lin–Wei
  independence sandwich as the fallback when only weights are given.
- **Per-covariate design effects**, defined against the same weighted fit with
  every subject as their own PSU — so the number is about clustering, not about
  weighting.
- **`lonely_psu_strata` on every result.** A single-PSU stratum contributes
  nothing to the variance; that is standard and it is a silently narrowed
  interval if nobody is told. It is 0 here.
- **`wald_test`.** Because a likelihood ratio is **not valid** against a weighted
  pseudo-likelihood, `likelihood_ratio_test` now raises on a weighted fit rather
  than returning a p-value from the wrong reference distribution.

### What it changed

**Every hazard ratio got larger.** The DNAm subsample under-represents the
higher-risk part of the population, so the unweighted analysis was the
conservative one:

| | unweighted | weighted |
|---|---|---|
| GrimAge2 HR/SD | 1.443 [1.361, 1.531] | **1.527** [1.410, 1.652] |
| PhenoAge HR/SD | 1.163 | **1.220** |
| Dysregulation HR/SD | 1.196–1.254 | **1.282–1.424** |
| Baseline C (age + sex) | 0.7394 | 0.7618 |
| Joint C | 0.7731 | 0.7943 |

**Both conclusions survive.** The trained-on hierarchy still reproduces
(mortality > phenotype > chronological age), and the same three clocks —
SkinBlood, Lin, Weidner — still carry no evidence over six routine blood tests.
Zhang and Vidal-Bralo weaken to p = 0.045 and 0.027 and should now be read as
marginal.

Design effects run 0.88–1.71 (median ≈ 1.15), so clustering costs little here and
almost all of the change is the weighting. That is worth stating because it is
the part a reader cannot guess.

### The estimator earns the extension

Three more checks in `tests/test_survival.py`, on the same principle as the
existing three:

1. **A defining property.** Weight 2 must give exactly the fit a duplicated row
   gives. This is what separates "the weight reached the risk set" from "the
   weight reached the event term".
2. **An identity one derivative out.** The per-subject score residuals that build
   the sandwich must sum to the gradient of the log pseudo-likelihood — at the
   fit and away from it. A mis-derived residual yields a plausible variance and
   fails only this.
3. **A planted design.** A population whose two strata have different hazard
   ratios, sampled at different rates. The weighted fit on the biased sample must
   land on the whole-population answer; the unweighted one must visibly miss it.

> **A test premise that was wrong, recorded because it is instructive.** The
> first clustering test made the *covariate* cluster-level and asserted the
> design SE would exceed the model SE. It came out 2× **smaller** — and that was
> correct. If the model is well specified, within-PSU score residuals are
> uncorrelated and a design effect below 1 is legitimate. What inflates a
> clustered variance is shared variation the model has *not* accounted for, so
> the test now plants an unmodelled PSU-level frailty.

## 4. The pooling estimator changed, and 1,164 claims went away

Not asked for; found while researching §6, and large enough to act on.

The reported confidence interval is now **Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman**, widened
to DerSimonian-Laird wherever HK comes out narrower (the modified rule, Röver et
al. 2015). DL substitutes an *estimated* τ² into the weights and then proceeds as
though it were known, so its interval is too narrow — worst when k is small, and
24% of this corpus pools fewer than ten contrasts.

| | DerSimonian-Laird | Reported now |
|---|---|---|
| Genes whose interval excludes zero | 4,875 | **3,711** |
| Median interval width | 0.843 | 0.928 |

**1,164 claims retracted, none created.** A wider interval can only take a
verdict away, and a corpus-scale property test asserts that rather than assuming
it.

Also new: a **95% prediction interval** on every pooled estimate, in the API, the
MCP payload, and drawn as a dashed rule behind the pooled diamond in the forest
plot. The confidence interval says where the *mean* effect is; the prediction
interval says where the *next study's* effect would fall. Median width 1.831 —
about twice the confidence interval. Conflating the two is the most common
misreading of a forest plot, and an agent reading a `no_evidence` verdict needs
the second to know what it would see if it ran the experiment.

**The living-evidence baseline was reset in the same commit**, deliberately. A
diff between a DL baseline and an HK rebuild would have published 1,164 retracted
claims as findings when no evidence moved. Snapshots now record `estimator`, and
`diff_snapshots` refuses to compare across two.

## 6. Research — where this should go next

Sources at the end. Ordered by value per unit of work, and the first two are
already built.

### 6.1 Done this session, from the research

- **Hartung-Knapp + prediction intervals** (§4). The HKSJ method "considerably
  outperforms" DL and is recommended-but-rarely-applied; prediction intervals are
  the standard recommendation alongside I² and are almost never reported. For a
  tool whose entire proposition is honest intervals, shipping the interval that
  is known to be too narrow was the largest single statistical gap.
- **Binder design-based Cox** (§3). The textbook method for NHANES survival, and
  the reason the weights were carryable but not applicable.

### 6.2 The next statistical layer, in order

1. **Nested random effects by series.** 32 contrasts from 27 Series share
   subjects, so the pool still slightly overstates independence. A three-level
   model (contrast within series) is the correct fix, changes published numbers,
   and is now cheap because the estimator-versioned baseline can absorb it. This
   is the last *correctness* debt in the pooling.
2. **Influence diagnostics per gene.** Leave-one-out, Baujat, and Cook's
   distance. For a gene pooled from 14 contrasts, "which one contrast is
   carrying this result" is a question a reader will ask and cannot currently
   answer. It is one extra pooled fit per study per gene — cheap to precompute
   for the ~3,700 genes that carry a verdict, and it turns the forest plot from
   a display into an audit.
3. **Small-study effects.** Egger's regression and a funnel plot for genes with
   k ≥ 10. GEO is not a publication-bias-free corpus — datasets get deposited
   because something was found — and this repo is better placed than most to say
   so, because it re-derives rather than extracts.
4. **Proportional-hazards diagnostics.** Schoenfeld residuals over 20 years of
   follow-up, where the assumption is least likely to hold. Currently listed as a
   limitation with nothing behind it.
5. **Cause-specific mortality.** `ucod_leading` is carried and unused. A clock
   that predicts cardiovascular death and not cancer death is a different claim
   from one that predicts "death", and the column is already in the frame.
6. **Weighted CSD on NHANES 2017–2018.** The one remaining result that is a
   claim about a sample rather than a population.

### 6.3 The next data layer

**ARCHS4 remains the biggest single quality upgrade** and the case has
strengthened: it is now >2M uniformly processed RNA-seq samples from GEO/SRA in
one HDF5, with `archs4py` for slice access. It removes the microarray-only
limitation *and* the uncorrected cross-study processing heterogeneity in one
move, because every sample went through one pipeline. Do it now rather than
later — the living-evidence machinery exists to diff it, and the estimator
version field means the two changes will not be confused for each other.

Second: a genuine **second omic layer**. `omic_layer` still reads
`transcriptome` on all 485,905 rows, which makes the column a promise rather than
a fact.

### 6.4 What the field is asking for, and what that implies

The Biomarkers of Aging Consortium's stated blockers are reproducibility across
cohorts, inconsistent dataset structures, and validation against hard clinical
end points. This repo now has all three. The 2026 conference themes are
explicitly *application of existing biomarkers to unique datasets* and
*combinations of biomarkers* — which is exactly what §2.3 of
`RESULTS_CROSSLAYER.md` does, and it is the part nothing else publishes.

The implication for what to build: **the comparison is the product.** Not "here
is GrimAge's hazard ratio", but "here is what GrimAge adds over a blood panel,
and for three clocks the answer is nothing." Two things follow:

- **Let a user bring their own cohort and get the same comparison.** The clock
  and resilience tabs already accept uploads; the nested-model comparison does
  not. That is the highest-value *feature* on this list.
- **Expose a benchmark surface.** Biolearn is the consortium's own harness for
  unified validation. A GeroQuery result that can be read as "how does this
  biomarker score against six routine blood tests on 75M person-equivalents"
  is the kind of number that gets cited.

### 6.5 Visualisation, in order of what it would change

The front end has four views and one real chart per view. What is missing is not
prettier charts; it is **charts that answer a question the tables cannot**:

1. **Prediction interval on the forest plot** — done this session. Highest impact
   per pixel: it makes visible that most genes' next study could land anywhere.
2. **Volcano over the whole corpus** — effect size against −log₁₀ q, coloured by
   verdict. The landscape view currently bins effect size alone, which hides
   that most large effects are also the least certain.
3. **Cross-species scatter** — human g against mouse g for orthologs, one point
   per gene. Both are computed and nothing shows the comparison as a comparison.
   This is the view most likely to produce a finding.
4. **Tissue small-multiples for one gene** — the data is per-tissue and the UI
   pools it. Half the human contrasts are skeletal muscle, and a reader cannot
   currently see that.
5. **Weighted survival curves by dysregulation quartile** — the mortality view
   has hazard ratios and no survival curve, which is the one chart every reader
   of a mortality result expects.
6. **A leave-one-out strip beside the forest plot**, once §6.2.2 exists.

## 9. Gotchas added this session

- **Do not diff a snapshot across estimators.** `panel_diff.ESTIMATOR` is pinned
  into every snapshot and `diff_snapshots` raises. Changing the estimator means
  resetting the baseline in the same commit with a changelog entry saying so.
- **Round before judging.** The exported verdict must be derived from the
  interval as it ships, not from full precision, or the two disagree at the
  fourth decimal. Add `0.0` to collapse negative zero — JavaScript reads `-0 < 0`
  as false. Bug #20.
- **`mcp` is capped below 2.0 on purpose.** Lifting the cap means moving FastAPI
  forward in the same change. Bug #21.
- **A likelihood ratio test is invalid on a weighted fit.** `wald_test` is the
  replacement; `likelihood_ratio_test` raises rather than answering.
- **Design effects below 1 are legitimate.** Stratification can buy information.
  A test asserting "clustering always widens" is asserting a false premise.
- **Pick regression-test fixtures by querying the corpus, not from the bug
  report.** Two of this session's tests passed against their own reintroduced
  bug until the fixture came from the data.
- **Playwright runs against `dist`, never the dev server.** Two of the four
  silent UI failures were in the exported Parquet.
